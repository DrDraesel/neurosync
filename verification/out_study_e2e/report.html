What cannot be concluded from this
A short single-condition spectral summary cannot support any clinical or psychiatric conclusion: no disorder, no treatment indication, no mental state. Clinical EEG interpretation uses engineered montages, provocation (eyes open/closed, hyperventilation, photic stimulation), artifact review by a trained reader, and comparison against normative databases — the "average brain" reference (for example qEEG z-scores, where a value at |z| ≥ about 2 lies outside the 95% band). This app's only references are the subject's own stored baseline and the numbers shown. For anything that matters medically, record a proper study and take it to a qualified clinician.

Reading method (for the clinician)

Method source. The qEEG sheet and these notes follow the reading method taught on the source channel below; the app re-implements the sheet layout and colour rule, not a licensed normative database.
Source: @supernormalbydrtrishleigh (Dr. Trish Leigh, Leigh Brain & Spine), reviewed 2026-11-03.
The five rows of the sheet.
RowWhat it means here
Absolute powerhow much energy (µV²) that band carries at that site — how "charged" it is.
Relative powerthat band's share of the total at that site — which of the five speeds dominates.
Amplitude asymmetryleft vs right difference at homologous sites.
Coherencehow similarly two sites behave in that band (shared information; too much = rigidity, too little = poor communication).
Phase lagthe timing offset between sites — how late or early the signal arrives.
Z-scores and colours. The sheet legend is a z-scale from −3 to +3: green = at the reference, blue = below, yellow → orange → red = above. In this app the z is always computed against the reference block printed with the sheet — never against an age-matched clinical database, because none is used here.
Reference block kind. Every sheet prints which reference its z-scores were computed against; a reference is never invented.
KindMeaning (printed with the sheet)
sessionself-referenced vs this session's channel mean
baselinevs this subject's saved baseline (date)
normvs local norm n=…, age … (a local file, only usable once 20 or more subjects exist and the age band is recorded)
noneraw values — no reference selected
A norm is a local file that only becomes usable once 20 or more subjects exist with the age band recorded; until then the app does not call it normative.
What the devices can and cannot support.
  • BrainBit (4 channels, O1/O2/T3/T4, shared reference) — screening-grade view at four sites: band powers, relative shares, two asymmetry pairs (O1/O2, T3/T4) and at most six coherence/phase pairs. Its four-site map is mostly extrapolation and is NOT a 19-channel qEEG. It can support a coarse spectral look at four sites; it cannot support a regional or whole-brain qEEG reading.
  • DragonEEG (21 scalp channels + 3 auxiliary) — qEEG-grade recording: the device the 5×5 sheet is designed for (21 sites, nine homologous asymmetry pairs, about 200 coherence/phase pairs). The headset is not a medical device and this app is not a medical device, so its output stays a descriptive research recording, not a clinical measurement.
These numbers can support: a descriptive spectral summary of the recorded sites; a within-subject comparison against that subject's own saved baseline of the same condition; documentation of recording conditions and measured signal quality.
They cannot support: a diagnosis, a screening or treatment decision, a mental-state claim, a comparison with an age-matched normative database, any conclusion beyond the recorded sites, or any conclusion from a single number or a single head — read clusters across rows and bands, and check the raw traces first (artifact looks like a region).
Interpretation is clinician-led. Nothing in this document interprets the numbers medically. Whether any number is clinically meaningful is for the clinician reviewing the original recording to decide; this section only explains the method the sheet uses.
